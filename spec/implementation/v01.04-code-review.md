## Findings

### #1 🟡 Redundant move-log reconstruction per `submit_move` (LOW) — deferred
**Concrete count:** `_handle_submit_move` calls `repo.reconstruct_game` directly, then
`repo.current_turn` (which internally calls `reconstruct_game` *again* for the same match), then —
after committing the move — `_current_state` opens a **new** session and calls `reconstruct_game` and
`current_turn` (which reconstructs a fourth time) once more. Each reconstruction replays the entire
move log through a fresh `TicTacToe`. At MVP scale (a single match, ≤9 moves, SQLite) this is
computationally irrelevant, but it's a real, avoidable pattern (e.g., threading the already-built
`game`/`board` through instead of re-deriving). Homed to v05.01 alongside the roadmap's other
resilience/perf polish, not fixed now.

## What's solid
The move-authority sequence matches §5.4 exactly and is naturally race-safe: because `current_turn`
is always re-derived from the *actually committed* move log (never cached), a concurrent out-of-turn
attempt is correctly rejected regardless of how the two connections' coroutines interleave — no
retry-loop was needed here the way `assign_symbol` needed one in v01.02. `apply_move` remains the sole
legality authority (malformed move payloads are rejected there, not re-validated in the handler).
`ConnectionManager.broadcast`'s serialize-once/prune-dead-socket design means one bad connection can't
block delivery to the room. Zero model calls anywhere.
